# MPU/LiDAR steering stability — 2026-10-30

## Behavior and scope

The updated Pi and S3 bridge use fast heading feedback and slower hallway
centering. MPU yaw rate can end a tap before overshoot, independent LiDAR scans
must confirm a gradual centering target, and nearest-object clearance can veto
a correction even when wall medians look clear. Physical accuracy still needs
measured floor trials.

```text
MPU calibrated heading + yaw rate -> heading correction / early release
LiDAR wall medians -> confirmed, rate-limited centering target
LiDAR nearest returns -> immediate nudge veto and existing PATH stops
                      -> bounded motor tap and existing endpoint accounting
```

Holding a course requires small corrections. Lateral recentering also requires
a brief heading offset and return toward FRONT; it cannot remain perfectly
straight while changing lateral position. The goal is to avoid unnecessary
alternation and overshoot. An object ahead still stops the route. This change
does not plan a detour around it.

## Findings and implementation

The previous decision used heading angle alone. Equal wheel speed restoration
is gradual after release: at 5600 steps/s, a 34% cut and configured restoration
acceleration of 4800 steps/s² imply roughly 0.40 s under an ideal model. This
explains the benefit of earlier release; it is not measured chassis response.
The centering target could also jump by several degrees on successive packets,
and wall medians could hide isolated side objects from steering checks.

- `MpuHeading.rateDps()` exposes calibrated, mounting-corrected gyro rate while
  valid, returning zero after expiry, fault, invalidation or pending reference.
  Integration, calibration and the 10 ms / 100 ms sample/gap limits are retained.
- Early release occurs when the actual heading error closes toward its target
  and `abs(error) - closing_rate * 0.25 s <= 1 degree`. It runs in the bridge
  loop as well as packet decisions. Prediction only releases/suppresses a tap;
  actual-angle hysteresis chooses direction. Prediction cannot initiate reversal.
- Centering needs three agreeing independent scans over at least 400 ms.
  Width changes beyond `max(15 cm, 10%)` or offset changes beyond 10 cm restart
  confidence. Confirmed estimates use a 0.25 low-pass update; target yaw changes
  at ≤2 degrees/s, capped ±4 degrees, with the existing 8 cm center deadband.
  Missing/inconsistent walls clear the target; reacquisition earns confidence.
- Direction confirmation also needs three independent scans spanning ≥400 ms.
  Only the first tap of a segment retains the ≥8 degree fast path. Reversals
  require full confirmation and the existing one-second cooldown. Each renewed
  correction starts its own confirmation interval. Startup grace/ramp count
  independent acquisitions rather than repeated snapshots.
- CF/CL/CR nearest-object fields bypass centering smoothing: the forward group
  suppresses nudges at ≤60 cm, and the requested turning side vetoes at ≤40 cm.
  Existing PATH stops remain, including normal front STOP at 95 cm and side
  STOP at 22 cm. Nearest fields still veto nudging when a GUI directional
  read-only control bypasses its PATH threshold.
- Tap strength remains 180–260 ms and 20–34% cut within existing executor caps.
  Earlier project notes record that much shorter taps gave no visible correction.
  Routes, wheel assignment, speed calibration and executor endpoints are unchanged.

Separating tracking from collision checks is also used in controllers such as
Nav2's regulated controller. GARBY retains its own bounded-tap implementation;
it does not adopt that planner or claim its collision model.
[Nav2 documentation](https://api.nav2.org/nav2-jazzy/html/md_nav2_regulated_pure_pursuit_controller_README.html).

## Coordinated Pi-to-bridge contract

The matching P/S transport sequence remains. Numeric steering now includes:

```text
S:<seq>|L=..|R=..|F=..|B=..|FL=..|FR=..|BL=..|BR=..|CF=..|CL=..|CR=..|A=<uint32>|T=..
```

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| L/R and diagonals | Existing wall/steering medians in cm |
| CF | Minimum nearest safety history over FRONT, FRONT_LEFT, FRONT_RIGHT |
| CL | Minimum nearest safety history over LEFT, FRONT_LEFT, BACK_LEFT |
| CR | Minimum nearest safety history over RIGHT, FRONT_RIGHT, BACK_RIGHT |
| A | Modulo-uint32 accepted source-acquisition identifier, separate from periodic P/S sequence |

Each clearance group is omitted if a constituent sector lacks current coverage.
Missing/invalid/duplicate clearance fields suppress nudging. A must be strict
uint32 decimal; repeats cannot earn confidence or refresh steering age. Backward
A is rejected even after an unusable scan with a valid newer ID. Ordering handles
uint32 wrap. Session/segment resets establish the proper acquisition epoch.

The Pi retains its 0.8 s source-age budget and clock checks; the bridge retains
its ingress/steering age budgets. Its 500 ms steering receipt-age check does not
mean 500 ms maximum source age. The producer caps S at 244 ASCII bytes and falls
back to same-sequence STABLE on overflow, leaving PATH independent. A normal
all-8-metre example with maximum uint32 P/A is 126 bytes.

Deploy the complete Pi directory and S3 sketch including `MpuHeading.h` and
`SteeringAssist.h`. Bridge build ID is `STRAIGHT-20261030`. Old numeric S packets
without A/CF/CL/CR cannot nudge on the new bridge. Keep current matching executor
firmware for heading segment notices, operator controls and SMS; executor source
is unchanged here. The synthetic simulator lacks the new evidence and cannot
establish acceptance of this steering behavior.

The existing 95 cm front/diagonal STOP envelope can stop the robot in a narrow
hallway before centering is permitted. Direct firmware fixtures with side walls
at 50/110 cm prove decision behavior only; they do not establish a production-
clear corridor. Thresholds must not be reduced without measured body/braking
clearance. Actual corridor width and LiDAR orientation matter.

For separately authorized hardware acceptance:

1. Lift wheels, keep a physical stop accessible and verify MPU sign and each
   correction's actual wheel/direction. Boot level/still, MPU +Z up. Confirm
   segment END, stale scan, MPU fault and emergency stop behavior.
2. Mark a reference line in a sufficiently wide clear corridor. At consistent
   speed, initial pose and payload, measure lateral deviation, peak heading
   error, corrections and reversals per metre. Repeat centered and offset to
   each side. Define pass limits from chassis footprint and clearance margins.
3. Use isolated soft test objects on left/right and diagonals outside the body
   envelope. Confirm nudge vetoes despite clear wall medians, and front STOP
   before contact. Include doorways and missing returns; neither may create
   abrupt lateral targets or substitute unknown evidence with clear.
4. Repeat with intended payload/battery conditions. Measure post-release yaw
   rate before tuning the 250 ms horizon. Change one parameter at a time and
   repeat stopping checks. A step counter cannot prove straight wheel motion.

Gyro yaw can drift and FRONT can begin misaligned. Eight sector summaries cannot
reliably distinguish a long flat object from a wall or estimate robust corridor
angle. Further improvement needs confidence-qualified line fitting from raw
LiDAR points and a measured footprint; encoders would add feedback absent from
the current open-loop drive. These changes do not guarantee zero zigzag or exact
position.
