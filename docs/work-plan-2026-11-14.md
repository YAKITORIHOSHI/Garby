## What the code does today

Checked in the source on 2026-11-14, so the plan changes the right places.

- **Moving objects.** `RasPi/motion_tracker.py` already tracks small LiDAR segments with a Kalman
  filter in the *ground* frame, removing the robot's forward speed reported by the executor
  (`[DRIVE:F:<cm/s>]`). It does **not** remove the robot's rotation. A heading correction of
  4 deg/s makes a still object 5 m away slide sideways at about 35 cm/s in the scan, which is
  exactly `MOVING_MIN_CM_S`. That is the "fire extinguisher looks like it moves" case.
  The tracks never reach the map: `MotionAssessment.tracks` is used only for stop/slow decisions.
- **Walls.** `RasPi/hallway_geometry.py` fits a left and a right wall from points 80 cm behind to
  160 cm ahead of the LiDAR (a 2.4 m baseline) and sends `WL/WR/H`. The executor fuses them only
  in `LONG_HALLWAY` steps. Read-only front/back does not remove `WL/WR/H`: wall fusion already
  works with the distance limits bypassed.
- **Station.** `OUTBOUND_ROUTE` starts `BUMPS 500, STRAIGHT 8000, TURN_LEFT 90`: the robot drives
  about 1.1 m straight out from the wall, then turns into the hallway. `RETURN_ROUTE` ends
  `TURN_RIGHT 90, BUMPS 500, STRAIGHT 7000, TURN_LEFT 180`: it drives at the wall and spins beside
  it. On that last leg the wall enters the 160 cm slow zone at once and the 95 cm front box after
  a few centimetres, and the forward sonar reads it too. The 2026-11-14 live run was held at the
  base by the rear wall and a wall step in the side band
  (`artifacts/manual-disposal-hold-2026-11-14/`).
- **Routes** are step lists (`MAIN_EXECUTOR_CODE/pointsRun.ino`), and `RouteResume.h` already

- **Headless.** `final_w_serial.py --headless` exists, and so do `garby-bridge.service.example` and

## Items 1 and 8 — objects and people in the hallway

One pipeline serves both requests. Item 8 finds everything standing between the walls, moving or
not. Item 1 decides which of those are likely people.

### What a 2D LiDAR can and cannot say

The LiDAR sees one horizontal slice at about chest height. A person is a curved cluster 25–60 cm
wide. It cannot see faces, legs or children below the plane, and a person standing perfectly
still looks like a bin or a post of the same width. So the labels are:

| Label | Meaning | Map |
| --- | --- | --- |
| likely person | person-sized, curved, and proven to move over the ground at walking or running speed | **red dot** with a short line showing direction |
| moving object | moves over the ground but is not person-shaped (cart, door leaf) | amber dot |
| object | stands between the walls, not moving | orange square |
| wall fixture | touches the wall line and does not move (extinguisher, post, door frame) | wall colour, not marked |

A red dot stays red while the track lives, including when the person stops walking. "Likely
person" is a display and report label. Motion keeps treating every object generically, so the
project still makes no injury-prevention claim (AGENTS.md is reworded to say exactly this).

### Add

- `RasPi/scan_frame.py` — converts one scan to robot-frame numpy arrays **once**. Today the
  sectors, `hallway_geometry`, `motion_tracker` and `lidar_map` each convert the same 700 rays
  themselves. This is also the first heat reduction (item 6).
- `RasPi/ego_motion.py` — the robot's own motion between two scans:
  - rotation from the change of the wall heading `H` between scans, and when no walls are fitted,
    from aligning the long structure segments of both scans;
  - forward speed from `[DRIVE]`, as now;

    common motion is an error in the robot's own motion estimate, not a crowd. It is removed and

    mover. This is the direct answer to the extinguisher concern.

  - every point is classed as wall (within 6 cm of a fitted wall line), beyond the wall (seen
    through a door or opening) or **interior**;

    width, and the free width left and right of it;
  - a static cluster touching the wall line (within 25 cm) is a wall fixture;

    margins.

## Item 2 — station legs ignore the station walls

### Design

A route step can carry a `STATION` flag. While a flagged step runs, **walls** stop holding the
robot; everything that is not wall keeps every gate.

1. **Executor** — `RouteStep` gets `flags`. At the start of each step, and every 2 s while it
   runs, the executor sends `[LEG:<route>:<step>/<count>:<op>:<flags>]`; at the end of a route, a
   fault or idle it sends `[LEG:0]`. This is telemetry, like `[DRIVE]`.
2. **Pi** — station mode is on only while a `STATION` leg notice is under 3.5 s old, the link is
   connected and the robot state is a route. In station mode, returns that belong to a long
   straight segment (at least 90 cm long, fitted within 2.5 cm) are wall. Wall returns are left
   out of the front box, the slow zone, the side bands, the side and front sector limits and the
   spin sweep, **down to 3 cm from the body outline** (`STATION_WALL_MIN_GAP_CM`). A wall nearer
   than that still stops the robot: the body would touch it. A person, a bin or a cart at the
   station is not a long straight segment and holds the robot exactly as today.
3. **Executor sonar** — the sonar cannot tell a wall from a box. In a `STATION` straight it knows
   how far the leg still has to go, so an echo is an obstacle only when it is nearer than the wall
   should be: `remaining travel + STATION_PARK_GAP_CM − 10 cm`. Until the parked gap is measured
   (D9), the fallback is the 25 cm immediate stop only.
4. **Turns in a station step** skip the wall alignment (`align=SKIP`, a path that exists): beside
   the station the robot faces across the hallway and no wall is parallel to it.
5. Pre-run centring and wall fusion are already off for these short legs. No change.

Station mode ends by itself when the notices stop (controller restart, link loss, emergency
stop), so a lost message can only return the robot to the stricter rules.

  beside `serviceDriveReport()`; `STATION_*` constants at the top of `HeadingControl.h` and

## Item 3 — reverse run

### Behaviour

After an emergency stop inside a route, the operator has a third choice beside **Release stop**
and **Continue run**: **Reverse to start**. It releases the stop and takes the robot back to the
start of the interrupted route, then leaves it at IDLE facing the way it started, ready for the
next calibration attempt.

The robot turns around on the spot, drives **forward** back along each leg for exactly the wheel
travel it had covered, mirrors each turn, and turns around again at the start. Every motion gate
of a normal route applies: fresh GO, no STOP, sonar, MPU heading, spin sweep before each turn.
The contract "every route segment drives forward" stays true.

- Travel to undo comes from the same record Continue uses: completed steps count in full, the
  interrupted straight counts the mean wheel travel since its start, calibration bumps count as
  plain travel, and the forward travel of pre-run centring sidesteps is included.
- Turn targets are absolute in the route heading frame: each leg is driven at its original
  heading plus 180 degrees. An emergency stop in the middle of a turn needs no special case.
- The return legs are plain gyro-held straights, with wall fusion on the long hallway. No
  pre-run centring. Station flags apply on the way back as well.
- A run that *finished* short is covered too: `[OUTBOUND COMPLETE]` or a parked return route
  keeps its record, so Reverse is offered there after an emergency stop as well.
- Limits, stated on the button's dialog: the record is in RAM, so a controller restart removes
  the option; the robot must not have been moved by hand; wheel slip on the way out is repeated
  on the way back, so the start point is reached to within the same error.

### Add / change / remove

- Add: `[OP:<id>:REVERSE]` on Pi (`operator_support.py` `ACTIONS`, gating as CONTINUE), bridge
  (the action allow-list near line 912 and its release handling) and executor; `REV=0|1` on
  `[OP STATUS:1:..]`; `RouteResume::reversePlan()` producing the mirrored step list;
  `runReverse()` in `pointsRun.ino`; executor state `REVERSING` with notices `[REVERSING]`,
  `[REVERSE COMPLETE]`; the Tk button with a confirmation dialog; the robot state `REVERSING`
  in Firebase and the app.
- Change: `RouteResume` keeps per-step travel for the whole route, not only the interrupted
  step; the buzzer drive pattern covers `REVERSING`; the Pi's state table and the return
  logic treat `REVERSING` as a route in progress.
- Remove: nothing.

## Item 4 — running straight, not tilted

### Likely causes, to be told apart with one floor log

"Centred but tilted" means the heading the robot holds is not the hallway direction, so it drifts
until the 5 cm centring threshold pulls it back, again and again. Three candidates:

| Cause | Sign in `[HEADING STRAIGHT ..]` and `S:` lines | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The LiDAR is mounted a degree or two rotated (`GARBY_LIDAR_YAW_OFFSET_DEG` was never measured), so every `H` carries that error | steady drift to one side between centring corrections, always the same side | learn the mounting angle (below) |
| `H` is noisy on the 2.4 m baseline, so the hallway axis wanders | tilt changes side at random | longer baseline (below) |
| The heading hold has a steady error (wheel trim) | `err` in `[HEADING STRAIGHT]` does not average zero | executor trim gain; only if the log shows it |

Phase 0 captures that log (`bash RasPi/run_bridge.sh 2>&1 | tee ~/garby-floor.log`). The two Pi
changes below are made regardless, because they help in every case and need no flash.

### Add / change

- **Front and rear wall points** (the owner's suggestion). A second wall fit over 3 m behind to
  5 m ahead, where the scan has the points. The angle error of a line fit falls with its length,
  so this should at least halve the noise of `H` (the test below sets that mark). The far fit is used only when it agrees
  with the near fit within 1.5 degrees and has enough inliers; doors and openings fail that test
  and fall back to the near fit. `WL/WR` stay from the near window: lateral position should be
  measured where the robot is. The packet format is unchanged, so no firmware change.
- **Mounting angle learned from drift.** While driving, the sideways drift per metre is the sine
  of the *true* heading error, whatever the controller is doing. Comparing that with the measured
  `H` over runs of at least 3 m gives the LiDAR's mounting error. The Pi applies it to `H`, moves
  it by at most 0.1 degree per window to at most ±3 degrees, logs it
  (`Wall heading offset learned: +1.3 deg`) and stores it in a small state file, so the owner
  can copy the value into `GARBY_LIDAR_YAW_OFFSET_DEG`.
- Works with front and back on read-only, as wall evidence already does. A test pins that down.
- Executor, only if the log points there: raise `AXIS_GAIN` use of the now quieter `H`, or the
  trim gain. This would be an executor flash and is merged into phase 3.

### Remove

- The `T=` tilt value in the `S:` packet is parsed and ignored by the bridge. It stays for now
  (old bridges require nothing of it); it is noted for removal at the next protocol change.

### Tests

- `test_hallway_geometry.py`: far fit noise against near fit on simulated walls with 1.5 cm range
  noise (expect under half the standard deviation); a door opening, a post and a person beside
  the wall reject the far fit; near and far disagree → near only.
- New `test_yaw_offset.py`: a simulated run with a 2 degree mounting error converges to it within
  20 m and never exceeds its bounds; no walls or a stopped robot learns nothing.

## Item 5 — mobile app

### Cloud contract first (Pi side, phase 4)

| Path under `devices/<id>/` | Written by | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `commands/operator` | app | `{version, requestId, requestedAt (server time), requestedBy, action, status: pending}` |
| `commands/operator` (same node) | Pi | `status: claimed → accepted / failed / unconfirmed`, `reason` |
| `operator` | Pi | stop latched and confirmed, Continue available, Reverse available, sonar on/off, front/back read-only, hold reason, active zones, station leg, person and object counts, link state |
| `map/lease` | app | server timestamp, renewed every 20 s while the map screen is open |
| `map/frame` | Pi | one compact frame, only while a lease is under 45 s old |

- Actions: `ESTOP, RELEASE, CONTINUE, REVERSE, SONAR_ON, SONAR_OFF, FRONT_READONLY_ON/OFF,
  BACK_READONLY_ON/OFF, REBOOT`. The Pi passes each through the **same** function the Tk buttons
  call (`request_operator_action`, `safety_checks_changed`), so the phone can never do what the
  monitor cannot. Manual disposal and return keep their existing nodes.
- Age limits: an `ESTOP` is honoured up to 120 s old (it only ever stops). Every other action is
  refused when older than 20 s, so a release or continue can never fire late after a reconnect.
- Speed: the Pi listens to `commands/operator` on a stream instead of the 2 s poll, with a 1 s
  poll as fallback. The phone stop is a convenience, **not** a safety device: it depends on two
  internet links. The app says so on the button's help line.
- Read-only toggles from the phone work only when the Pi's existing
  `ALLOW_RUNTIME_SAFETY_DISABLE` permits them, and they are off again after every restart.
- Rules: only UIDs listed under `operators/<uid>` may write `commands/operator` and `map/lease`;
  the example rules file in the app repository is extended. Deploying rules changes the live
  database, so the owner does it or approves it.
- Map frame, about 1.2 kB: 360 distances (one per degree, centimetres, 16 bit, base64), wall
  lines, up to 12 objects with kind and velocity, active zones, path codes, acquisition age.
  2 frames per second, 1 when the Pi is hot. About 9 MB per hour per open map screen, far inside
  the free Firebase allowance. No frame is written when nobody is watching.

## Item 6 — heat

1. **Measure before changing** (phase 0). New `tools/thermal_profile.py`: every 5 s, CPU
   temperature, frequency, throttle flags, fan state and CPU time per thread of the bridge, the
   LiDAR driver and the X server, written to CSV. Four 10-minute runs: idle; bridge with the Tk
   monitor over `ssh -Y`; bridge with a local display; bridge headless. This shows what the heat
   comes from instead of guessing.
2. **Expected largest saving: no Tk.** The radar map and its sweep animation are redrawn
   continuously, and over `ssh -Y` every draw is encrypted and sent. Headless (item 7) removes
   all of it.
3. **Processor**
   - one scan conversion instead of four (`ScanFrame`, phase 1);
   - map frames built only while a phone holds a lease;
   - the Tk sweep animation off by default, and the monitor's redraw limited to new scans;
   - Firebase: command streams instead of several polling threads; heartbeat unchanged;
   - LiDAR driver log output stays discarded.
4. **Memory.** 1.2 GB of 4 GB is in use, so memory is not the heat source. Still: bounded
   queues everywhere new, arrays reused between scans, `gc.freeze()` after start-up so the
   collector stops rescanning long-lived objects.
5. **Hot behaviour.** At 75 °C the Pi halves the map and telemetry rates and logs it; at 80 °C
   the existing warning and SMS notice apply. Nothing on the path-decision side is ever slowed.
6. **Operating system and hardware** (owner's actions, need sudo or a screwdriver): fan curve in
   `config.txt`, no desktop session at boot for headless use, check that the active cooler is
   seated. Listed with exact lines in the deployment notes.
7. **ESP32s.** The executor already drops to 80 MHz in a long hold. If D4 says a controller or a
   motor driver is the hot part, that is a separate hardware question.

Test: the same four profiles after the changes; report average temperature and CPU share before
and after. Scan-callback time per scan is measured in a unit benchmark with a recorded scan
(`tools/bench_scan.py`), with a pass mark of no slower than today.

## Risks and limits

- **People.** A still person is not marked red until they move, a child below the scan plane is
  not seen by the LiDAR at all, and the label is a best guess. The low sonar and the existing
  stop zones remain the protection.
- **Station legs** depend on a chassis outline that has never been measured. With the default
  60 x 60 cm outline the 3 cm wall floor may be wrong in either direction (D9).
- **Reverse run** returns to the start only as well as the wheels repeated their steps. It is a
  calibration convenience, not a positioning system.
- **Phone control** depends on Wi-Fi on the robot and internet on the phone. A stop sent from the
  phone can arrive late or not at all.
- **Kinematic model.** Firmware host tests cannot show straightness, slip or stopping distance.
  Items 2, 3 and 4 are finished only after a floor run.
- **Firmware state.** CONTINUE-20261114 is itself not yet uploaded or accepted. Phase 3 builds on
  it; a fault found on the floor could belong to either change, so the bench steps of both are
  run in order.
- **Message size.** New `S:` fields must keep the packet under 244 bytes; the plan adds none.
  New executor notices travel the existing notice path and are checked against the bridge's
  line-length limit in the host tests.
