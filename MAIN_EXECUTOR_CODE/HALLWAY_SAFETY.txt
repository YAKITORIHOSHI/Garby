Hallway seats and motion reliability — 2026-10-29

Repairs in this change:
- Clear-sonar confirmation and the median filter restart after invalid readings
  or a sample gap longer than 180 ms. Old evidence cannot release an obstacle.
- Straight, adjustment and turn loops check each wheel's software step counter.
  A running counter with no change for 2 seconds stops both motors and latches
  ROUTE FAULT. Steering retargets do not refresh this timeout. Stops/waits and
  legitimate resumptions restart monitoring; a completed wheel is not a stall.
- Existing STOP priority, path expiry, MPU heading reference preservation during
  obstacle waits, and remaining-route-target recovery remain in place.

Handling seats with the existing route:
LiDAR checks the route and can veto MPU steering. The servo keeps the ultrasonic
sensor looking ahead while moving, providing an independent close-object stop.
MPU maintains the straight-segment heading; it does not measure free space.
Keep these roles separate: turning the sonar away during travel removes its
forward coverage, and steering toward a single long side range does not prove
that the robot's whole body fits between a chair and the wall.

For now the robot stops, continues sampling, reports/beeps while blocked and
resumes the remaining target after clearance. A stationary chair can therefore
keep it waiting indefinitely by design. This is not an automatic chair bypass.
Existing LiDAR radial thresholds can also stop in narrow halls; do not lower
these solely to get past chairs without measuring body clearance and braking.

Automatic bypass needs overall body width/length (including projections), sensor
heights/orientation, minimum available gap, measured braking distance and a way
of recovering route position after the detour. A proposed next design is:
stop -> stationary servo sonar sweep + fresh LiDAR body-clearance assessment ->
bounded, MPU-monitored maneuver -> verify corridor and route re-entry.
If measurements are unavailable or neither side fits, remain stopped and report
blocked. A scan's apparent gap alone must not authorize a turn or blind reverse.
No bypass behavior or new scan/maneuver protocol was implemented in this change.

Limits and acceptance:
The new watchdog detects a stuck software pulse executor, not a mechanically
stalled wheel that still receives pulses. There are no wheel encoders here.
MPU yaw alone cannot prove forward travel or correct step loss. Chairs' legs,
seat overhangs and surfaces outside the LiDAR/sonar sensing height can be missed.
Verify sensor placement physically; no code-only change eliminates those gaps.
Test wheels lifted first: each-wheel executor fault, STOP during turns, link
loss, and sonar dropout. Then supervise chair/leg/overhang tests at low speed,
measure loaded stopping distance, and verify route completion after clearance.
No firmware was flashed and no physical motion was tested in this session.
A latched motion fault needs supervised recovery from a known route position.
